Triage Framework Reference

The framework used to score all inbound requests. Each indicator is scored 1–5 by Claude Sonnet, with a transparent rationale provided for each decision. The goal is better-informed human decisions, not automated accept/reject.

Six Triage Indicators
01
Request Completeness
Degree of detail and structure in the inbound request, signalling readiness to progress. A high score indicates the requester has provided clear scope, timeline, budget, and contact details. A low score indicates the request is too vague to act on without further information.
1 — No actionable information5 — Fully specified
02
Strategic Alignment
Fit with FDE team priorities, capability areas, and firm-level strategic objectives. High alignment means the request directly maps to FDE capability areas: Harvey workflow builds, agentic pipeline design, RAG and document intelligence, or client-facing AI prototyping for Deals contexts.
1 — Not a Deals or FDE use case5 — Directly on roadmap
03
Complexity and Effort
Estimated scope, technical difficulty, and resource requirements based on comparable prior work. This indicator informs capacity planning — a high complexity score does not reduce priority, but it does affect how many FDEs need to be allocated and for how long.
1 — Trivial, hours of work5 — Large multi-week build
04
Commercial Profile
Revenue potential and client relationship value for external requests, or strategic internal weight for internal requests. External engagements with confirmed budget and a named senior sponsor score highest. Internal requests from the GenAI team on roadmap items also score highly.
1 — No commercial value5 — Major external engagement
05
Reuse Potential
Degree to which prior proposals, codebases, or engagement patterns from the proposal library can accelerate delivery. A high score means the FDE team has done something near-identical before and has reusable assets — reducing time to delivery significantly.
1 — No precedent exists5 — Near-identical prior engagement
06
Time Sensitivity
Urgency of response required, driven by client deadlines, competitive dynamics, or internal commitments. A live deal deadline within days scores 5. A flexible exploratory conversation with no fixed date scores 1. High urgency requests should be reviewed same-day regardless of total score.
1 — No urgency, flexible5 — Live deadline within days
Recommended Actions
ACCEPT
Accept and prioritise
The request is sufficiently complete, strategically aligned, and commercially viable. The FDE team should respond within 24 hours with a proposed scope and timeline. High urgency accepts should be escalated immediately.
CLARIFY
Request further information
The request shows potential but lacks the detail needed to make a confident triage decision. The FDE team should respond promptly requesting specific missing information before committing capacity. The qualification view will specify exactly what is needed.
DEFER
Defer — low priority or capacity constrained
The request is legitimate but not urgent, and the team's current capacity is better directed elsewhere. The request should be acknowledged, logged, and revisited when capacity allows. Typically used for low urgency internal requests with no fixed deadline.
REFER
Refer out — wrong team or misaligned ask
The request does not fall within FDE's capability area or is better suited to another team. The qualification view will specify who to refer to. Common referral destinations include Consulting (for non-Deals AI use cases), Tech Catalyst (for production engineering), or the relevant BU Accelerator Unit.
About this system

This triage system ingests inbound FDE demand from three live channels — Gmail (via API), Microsoft Teams (JSON), and Otter.ai phone transcripts — plus direct entry via this interface. Each request is normalised into a consistent structured format, enriched with matched prior proposals from the FDE engagement library, and scored by Claude Sonnet against the six triage indicators above. The output is a ranked prioritised queue designed to support faster, more consistent, and better-informed human decisions on where the FDE team invests its capacity. All final decisions remain with the FDE team — this system qualifies, it does not decide.
